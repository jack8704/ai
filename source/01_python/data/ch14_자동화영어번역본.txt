To prevent further damage, Geoje City activated the Disaster and Safety Countermeasures Headquarters and put all employees on emergency duty starting the morning of the 17th. 208 households and 335 residents in the risk area were urgently evacuated to safer areas such as schools and village halls, and the government has issued the national firefighting mobilization order three times to focus firefighting resources on large-scale rescue operations and flood recovery efforts to manage the damage along the southern coast. As of the 18th, most of the previously flooded roads have been closed, and bus services and other traffic have returned to normal, but the roadbeds are still tangled with washed-up soil and debris, and many areas have suffered asphalt damage or ground subsidence, so it is expected to take a considerable amount of time to fully restore the roads. Geoje City is mobilizing all available personnel and heavy equipment to prioritize the restoration of facilities closely related to traffic and residents’ daily lives, and is significantly strengthening emergency safety inspections and surveillance activities with experts, focusing on steep slopes and hazardous areas where the ground has weakened, to prevent further landslides and secondary damage. In addition, Geoje City strongly urged citizens who suffered damage to their private property due to flooding or damage to homes, as well as to small business sites and agricultural and livestock facilities, through the relevant community service center or the National Disaster Safety Portal within 10 days. If you report after recovery has already begun or after the deadline, it may be difficult to verify the facts, and you may be excluded from receiving support.
